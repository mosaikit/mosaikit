---
id: MK-056
title: "Permissions declared by extensions, granted through profiles, groups and people"
area: security
priority: P1
status: done
decisions: [ADR-0038]
acceptance:
  - A permission declared in the manifest of an extension appears in the profile editor of the
    organization and is off until granted.
  - A member whose profile lacks a permission gets 403 from the API of the extension, even when the
    UI is bypassed.
  - Granting the permission to a group of the member, or to the member as an exception, makes the
    same request succeed.
  - An invitation that chooses role, profile and groups shows the resulting permissions before it is
    sent.
---

Three fixed roles are not enough to say what each person may do in each extension. Extensions
declare their permissions in the manifest, and new ones arrive switched off; organizations bundle
them into profiles assigned to roles, and add them through groups or as personal exceptions, without
code. The kernel checks them on navigation, routes and API alike, so that an extension backend
cannot be reached around the UI.

## History

- 2026-10-13: written from the needs of a product built on Mosaikit, reviewed from its backlog; 6 of
  its requirements depend on it.
- 2026-10-13: in progress — public API proposed for review (PR #42).
- 2026-10-13: API reviewed and ADR-0038 accepted: permissions are named `<extension>:<permission>`
  outside their extension, administrators of the organization hold every permission and guests none
  unless granted explicitly. The apps per role (MK-030) move onto permissions in the implementation,
  which also brings the `@mosaikit/sdk` counterpart and the REST API.
- 2026-10-13: the kernel implements the `Permissions` bean, profiles (one per member, assigned or the
  default profile of members), grants to teams and personal exceptions, with the REST API for
  administrators (including the effective permissions with their sources and a preview for
  invitations) and `GET /api/v1/shell/permissions`; the console UI, the invitation preview in the
  console and the apps per role on permissions follow.
- 2026-10-13: done — the console of the organization has Permissions (profiles, people with their
  exceptions and effective permissions by source, teams) and the invitation of a person with role,
  profile and teams, which shows the resulting permissions before it is sent; the administrators of
  the organization list the members and give profile and teams to those who already are, while
  adding people stays with platform administrators until MK-068. Every app has the permission
  `use-<app>`, and the apps per role (MK-030) became apps restricted to who holds it; guests see an
  app only when granted its permission. The shell hides contributions that name
  a permission the person lacks. End-to-end test `22-permissions` covers the four criteria with the
  permission `export-notes` of the fixture Notes.
- 2026-10-14: `PermissionHolders` in kernel-api (#104): who holds a permission in the organization,
  people and teams, by the rules of `Permissions`, also for a subscriber of events without a person,
  and the published view `mk_published.permission_holder` (migration V33); tests in
  `PermissionHoldersTest`.
- 2026-10-14: a change of profiles, team grants and exceptions raises an event of the platform
  (MK-085, #103).
- 2026-10-14: a guest given a permission or a resource of an extension reaches its API, where these
  rules answer for them (MK-032, issue #92).
